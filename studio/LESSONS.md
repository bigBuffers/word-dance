# Lessons from the juries — read before you plan or draw

Sixteen cards drawn four different ways were judged blind against the approved set (8 = approved standard). The best
recipe averaged 7.5; what cost points was never the light, the palette or the plain lettering — those were at the
approved level everywhere — but the items below. Every one of them was seen on a real card.

## The scene

1. **Nobody smiles at the camera.** Every figure looks at what it is doing or at the other figure: the kite, the leaves,
   the page, the bowl, grandma. The cast's eyes are solid ovals, so gaze = shift the whole features group 3–5 px toward
   the target and tilt the head a little; closed happy eyes (arcs) are fine for laughing. A figure that poses to the
   viewer is the single most frequent reason a card was marked down.
2. **The thing that carries the meaning is big and in the light**: a kite of at least 150 px, a clear gap of air and a
   shadow under a jumper, buns you can see in an open steamer, shelves that still read as books. If the direction's key
   object ends up small, enlarge it before anything else.
3. **Poses are asymmetric and say one thing.** No stiff parallel legs, no symmetric raised arms, no diamond legs under a
   jumper (tuck the knees forward), no hovering tiptoe (the toes touch the ground and carry a contact shadow). With the
   cast's arm length a raised hand reaches only the cheek: for reaching up, shading the eyes, a finger on the lips,
   lengthen the upper arm up to 20 % and bend the elbow outward, so the gesture cannot be read as a wave, a salute or
   wiping the nose. A finger on the lips lands ON the mouth, from the same side as the arm.
4. **Stay on model.** Children have no eyelids, lashes or red lips; adults use the same eyes (the women of the family cast
   carry the one small lash tick per eye that `cast/family/CAST-family.md` draws — add nothing more). Do not invent a new face.
5. **The ground, the table, the wall are finished surfaces**, never an empty slab: soft light patches, a few tiles or
   planks fading out, tufts, a contact shadow under everything. But keep them calm where the word stands.
6. **A prop must be read in one second and must not read as something else.** Seen: steamers without lids as pancake
   stacks, a reel as a doughnut, a ring-shaped shadow as a hole, a cushion as a swim ring, lift marks as dripping water
   or little legs, steam lines as wires, a hatch as a television, rumble marks plus a covered nose as a bad smell. A cast
   shadow is ONE soft ellipse. Steam is a soft cloud with one or two short curls, never long scribbles.
7. **No mechanical rows** (identical trees, evenly spaced windows) — vary size and spacing. No generic sparkles.
8. **At most six props**, and none cropped into the word's zone.
9. **Bright and crisp**: no brown or dark corners, no haze over the middle plane; blur belongs to the far plane only.

## The word

10. **Ghost (silent) letters**: fill in the chunk colour at 35–40 % opacity over the ground — it must still look like the
    chunk colour (salmon for coral, not sand or khaki; if the ground swallows it, put a soft cream glow behind it) — and a
    dashed keyline (`stroke-dasharray="7 6"`, width about 3.5) in the full chunk colour, no cream keyline, no shadow.
    `tools/letters` now gives overlap-free outlines, so the dashes no longer double up inside `e`, `a`, `k`, `t`, `f`.
11. **Clearance**: at least 24 px of calm ground between the word (its keyline) and any prop, cushion, shoe or hook
    object. A hook object that belongs to a letter (the bowl behind the `u`) stands clearly ABOVE the x-height with a gap,
    never perching on the letter like an accent, and never in a chunk colour.
12. **Hook marks must read as what they are at phone size** (360 px wide). Two tiny bars beside a dot read as brackets,
    teardrops as water. If a mark does not read, leave it out: the caption carries the hook.
13. **Centre the word in its zone**; do not push it to one side and leave half the table empty.
14. Contrast: coral / indigo / green letters with the cream keyline on a LIGHT, calm zone; never coral on a red track or
    indigo on a deep-blue floor.

## From the first ten of the hundred (2026-11-13: three judges, ten cards drawn from fixed art direction)

Lettering, light and palette were at the approved level on all ten; eight of ten were sent back for the figure work.
Every item below was seen on a real card of this run.

15. **Gaze, again — above all the ADULT and the second figure.** On seven of ten cards somebody still looked out at the
    viewer: the parent, the teacher, the onlooker, the cat. The adult looks at the child or the thing; the child looks at
    the adult who speaks to it. A front head with centred features IS a look at the viewer. Use the turned heads
    (`CAST.md`, `CAST-family.md` §10) or shift the features and tilt the head, and check each face in a zoom.
16. **Draw what the caption says.** `scene_zh` is shown beside the picture: the bite out of the ice cream, the tilted
    asking head, the arms round both shoulders, the head turned away, the curtain half drawn — each named gesture must be
    visible, and big enough to read at phone size.
17. **Folded poses are articulated, never blocks.** A sitter shows thighs on the seat, bent knees, shins, feet on the
    ground. A croucher shows a raised knee and a shoe under the hem — a legless skirt mound reads as a beanbag. A wader
    has two legs entering the water. A child riding on shoulders has thighs over the shoulders, knees, shins with skin
    and socks, and is held at the SHINS, not by the shoes. Parents sitting in grass show a lap or knees. Adults are
    visibly taller and broader in the shoulders than the child beside them.
18. **Depth order as directed**: "between and behind" means the child is overlapped BY the parents, not laid over them.
    People who talk to each other stand or sit close; an inviting hand points at the seat, not at empty floor.
19. **A light effect must not become an object.** Seen: a window reflection as a stack of dashes (a ladder), a halo
    behind ghost letters as a second moon or a spotlight, sun glitter as a dotted column, a vine's shadow as a damp
    stain, a white inner blouse as a post. One or two soft smears; small, soft halos; no hard-edged light shapes.
20. **No mechanical strips**: a saw-tooth row of identical grass blades, a brick or tile grid across the whole card,
    evenly spaced seeds or dashes. Break them up, vary them, fade them out.
21. **Bystanders act too.** No frontal figure staring out of a doorway; turn it to its own business. 路人 who are the
    subject (people, man, woman) get the sun-side rim and the same shadow hue as the main figure, and read as who the
    caption says (the woman with the basket must read as a woman).
22. **Ghost letters on a night sky**: a low-opacity gold over a pale halo turns khaki. Use a pale tint of the chunk
    colour at 55–60 % with the dashed keyline in the light full colour, on a halo barely lighter than the sky.
23. **Chunk gaps stay 8–16 px keyline to keyline** — also round a one-letter chunk; 30 px of air makes `fam i ly`.
24. **A building seen from outside needs an eave, a corner shadow, a ground line.** A hard vertical seam between sky and
    wall reads as two pictures side by side.
25. **On a number card nothing else comes in a countable group** (three vine strands on the card `two`).
